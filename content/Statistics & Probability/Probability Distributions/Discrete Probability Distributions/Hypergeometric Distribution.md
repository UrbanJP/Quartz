Consider a collection of $n$ objects. The number of objects in the collection of type 1 is $q$. Then the number that are not type 1 is $n-q$. Say we make a random selection of size $s$ without [[Replacement]]. The [[Random Variable]] $X \sim \text{Hypergeometric}(n,q,s)$ representing the number of objects of type 1 in the selection follows the hypergeometric distribution.


## Formal Definition
We say that $X$ is hypergeometrically distributed if the [[Probability Mass Function]] is
$$P(X=x) = \frac{\binom{s}{x} \binom{n-q}{n-x}}{\binom{n}{s}}$$
