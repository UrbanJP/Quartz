In financial markets, short selling is essentially the opposite of buying a financial product. If you expect the market to go up, you buy a stock to then sell it at a higher price. Short selling is done when the market is expected to fall.

How can you sell something you don't own? Don't we have to buy the stock first to sell it? In order to short the market your [[Broker]] borrows shares from a shareholder at an [[Annual Percentage Rate (APR)]] then sells the shares at current market price and pocketed. A contract is made by the lender and you so that you are legally bound to giving the same amount of shares back. If the market price goes down, you buy the same amount of shares that you borrowed and return them to the lender closing the contract and making a profit. If the market price goes up you pay more than you sold the shares for and have to give them to the lender resulting in a loss.
