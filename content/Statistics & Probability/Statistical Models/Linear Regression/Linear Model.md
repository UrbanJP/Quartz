In statistics, a linear model is any model constructed with variables called [[Predictor Variable|Predictors]] to estimate a variable called the [[Response Variable]]. For ease, linear models are typically constructed in the form
$$Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_1 + \beta_2X_2 + \ldots + \beta_nX_n + \varepsilon$$
The reason linear models are called linear is because of the [[Parameter|Parameters]] and not the variables.


## Examples
$$ \begin{matrix} \text{Linear Models} & \text{Non-Linear Models} \\
Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_1 + \beta_2X_2 + \varepsilon
& Y = \beta_0 \beta_1 X_1 + \varepsilon
\\ \\
Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_1 + \varepsilon
& Y = \log\beta_1X_1 + \beta_2X_2 + \varepsilon \\ \\
Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_1X_2X_3 + \beta_2 \log X_4 + \varepsilon
& Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1X_1^{\beta_2} + \varepsilon
\end{matrix}$$
