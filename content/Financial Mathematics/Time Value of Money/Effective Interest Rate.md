Effective interest rate is an [[Interest Rate]] of one period of time converted to another period of time. This leads to both being of equivalent value over the same total elapsed time. This is not the same as [[Nominal Interest Rate]]. It is also not to be confused with the [[Accumulation Factor]].

An interest rate as a [[Rate]] with respect to where we started.

Types include effective annual interest rate (EAR), effective monthly interest rate, and effective interest rate over any specified period.

## Formal Definition
For times $t_1 < t_2$ the general effective interest rate formula is
$$i_{[t_1, t_2]} = \frac{A_K(t_2) - A_K(t_1)}{A_K(t1)} = \frac{a(t_2) - a(t_1)}{a(t_1)}$$
where $A_K(t)$ and $a(t)$ are the [[Amount Function]] and [[Accumulation Function]] respectively. We can derive the effective annual rate (EAR) from this general formula. The effective annual rate formula is
$$\text{EAR} =(1+ \frac{i}{n})^n -1$$
where $i$ is [[Nominal Interest Rate]] and $n$ is [[Compounding Frequency]].

## Examples
Say we have an interest rate credited monthly at a rate of 0.8%. The effective annual interest rate would be $(1 + 0.008)^{12}= (1.008)^{12} = 1.1$ which translates to a 10% annual effective interest rate.
