The existential quantifier $\exists$ points out at least one element in a domain. It is often translated as "there exists", "there is", or "there is at least one".

The unique existential quantifier $! \exists$ points out **exactly** one unique element in a domain. It is often translated as "there exists only one", "there exists a unique", etc.

## Examples
$\exists x \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $x=5$
- There is a natural number $x$ such that $x=5$
- $\mathbb{N}$ is the [[Set]] of [[Natural Numbers]]
$! \exists a \in A$ such that $a \in \{ x \, | \, x \text{ is the multiplicative inverse of } 5 \}$
- There exists only one $a$ in the set $A$ such that $a$ is in the set of all multiplicative inverses of 5
