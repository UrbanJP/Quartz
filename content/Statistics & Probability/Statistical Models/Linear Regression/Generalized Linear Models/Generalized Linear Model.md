A generalized linear model is a generalization of an ordinary [[Linear Regression]] model. Linear regression operates by using one or more [[Predictor Variable|Predictor Variables]] to predict the [[Response Variable]]. Because it is linear, a constant change in the predictor means a constant change in the response, but this assumption does not always work in the real world. 

For example, let's say a model predicts that a drop of 10 degrees in temperature means that 100 less people will be at the beach that day. This model cannot account for very large beaches where maybe thousands of people go each day, nor can it account for small beaches that have less than 100 people on hot days. This model will lead to illogical areas like negative people being at the beach. Instead if we treat the model as a function of the response (?) we can better model the situation.
