The accumulation function $a(t)$ represents the worth of $1 at time $t$. In general $a(t) = A_1(t)$ where $A_K(t)$ is the [[Amount Function]]. The following formula utilizes both the amount and accumulation function
$$A_K(t) = K \cdot a(t)$$
$$A_K(t) = K \cdot A_1(t)$$
where $K$ is the [[Principal]].

In order to be a valid accumulation function, the function must equal $1$ when $t=0$, be [[Monotonicity|Monotonically Increasing]], and always positive. It's also important to know that $a(t)$ is not necessarily growth over any span of time $t$. The growth from years 0 to 3 can be different from years 2 to 5 even though they have the same difference.
## Examples
Suppose we have $1000 that grows into $1200 after two years. We then have the following equation:
$$K \cdot a(t) = 1000 \cdot a(2) = 1200$$
From this we can solve for $a(2)$ to get
$$a(2) = 1.2$$
