The discount $\nu(t)$ is the [[Reciprocal]] of the [[Accumulation Function]]. It measures how much $1 at a future time $t$ is worth right now, also known as the [[Present Value]].


## Motivation
Getting $K today is worth more than getting $K at a future time $t$. This is because if you invest $K right now, after $t$ years pass you'll have $A_K(t)$ dollars where $A_K(t)$ is the [[Amount Function]]. In particular, $1 will grow to $a(t)$ dollars in $t$ years. Since $A_K(t) = K \cdot a(t)$, if we invest $K=\frac{1}{a(t)}$ then $\frac{1}{a(t)}$ grows to $1 in $t$ years. This motivates the discount function $$\nu(t) = \frac{1}{a(t)}$$
