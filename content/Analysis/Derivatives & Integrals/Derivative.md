For a function $f(x)$, the derivative of the function quantifies how much the output is changing at each point. Specifically, it finds how much the output changes with a small adjustment to the input. It is also the slope of the [[Tangent Line]] at a [[Point]].

We can consider derivatives of functions as a whole or derivatives of functions at a point. The first case yields a function as a result. The second case yields a number specifically.

## Formal Definition
### Limit Definition
The derivative $L$ of a function $f(x)$ at a point $a$ is equal to the [[Limit]]
$$\lim_{h \to \infty} \frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{a+h-a}$$


### Function Definition
The derivative is a [[Arity|Unary]] [[Function]] with a domain of [[Differentiable]] functions and a range of [[Riemann Integral|Integrable]] functions.
