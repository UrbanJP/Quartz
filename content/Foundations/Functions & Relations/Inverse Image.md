The inverse image (or preimage) of a [[Set]] $A$ is the set of all values in the [[Domain]] that outputs a value in $A$. The inverse image is a [[Subset]] of the domain and $A$ is a subset of the [[Codomain]].

The essential question to ask for understanding the inverse image of $A$ under a function $f$ is "Which numbers get me a value in $A$ when I plug it into $f$?"

## Formal Definition
Let $f$ be a function $f:X \to Y$ and let $W \subseteq Y$. The inverse image of $W$ under $f$ is defined as $$\{x \in X | f(x) \in W \}$$
The notation for this is $f^{-1}(W)$ or $f^{-1}[W]$. 

Notice that the inverse image of $W$ under $f$ is just a subset of $X$.

## Examples
Let $f(x) = x+10$ and $g(x) = x + 100$. Additionally let $A = \{ 101, 102, 103 \}$. The inverse image of $A$ under $f$ is $\{ 91, 92, 93 \}$, but the inverse image of $A$ given $g$ is $\{ 1, 2, 3 \}$.
