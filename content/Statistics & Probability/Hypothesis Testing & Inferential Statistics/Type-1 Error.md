Type 1 error, is rejecting a true [[Null Hypothesis]]. It is thinking you have found a significant result, effect, difference, or pattern, but it turns out to be random chance.

The probability $\alpha$ of a type-1 error is chosen. This choice is known as the [[Significance Level]]. An initial thought may be to reduce the $\alpha$ as low as possible, but this has drawbacks. The main drawback is that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (probability of a [[Type-2 Error]]) are inversely [[Correlation|Correlated]]. A decrease in $\alpha$ increases $\beta$.

Compare with [[Type-2 Error]].

## Examples
Since it is standard practice to treat "no result" as the null hypothesis, in a test for a disease in patients, a type-1 error would be diagnosing a patient with a disease when they don't actually have one.
In reality, either the patient having no disease or the patient having a disease could be treated as the null hypothesis. It is simply the norm in medical statistics to treat not having a disease as the null hypothesis.
