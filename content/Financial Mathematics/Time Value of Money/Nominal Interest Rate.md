The [[Interest Rate]] without calculation of [[Compound Interest|Compounding]] or inflation. This makes the nominal rate distinct from both the [[Real Interest Rate]] and [[Effective Interest Rate]]. On occasions you may see the nominal rate notated as $i^{(m)}$ where $m$ is the [[Compounding Frequency]]. 

The compounding frequency is actually a necessity to define the nominal interest rate. A rate alone, such as 4%, doesn't make sense unless we know when it is calculated. So nominal rates are phrased in ways such as "4% compounded monthly", and "1.5% compounded annually".
