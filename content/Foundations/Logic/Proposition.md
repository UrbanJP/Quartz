A proposition is a statement that can be true or false. When analyzing arguments we consider propositions along with their [[Logical Connective|Logial Connectives]]. Here, proposition and statement are used interchangeably.

## Examples
The following are examples of propositions:
- The sky is blue
- All dogs are birds
- 2+2=5 
- For every set $A$ there exists a subset $B$ such that the [[Cardinality]] of $B$ is less than $A$
All of the above are either true or false

The following are examples of non-propositions
- Are you walking home today?
- Hooray!
- 2+2
- $f(x)$
- $\bigcup_{i \in I}$
- $\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ 
None of the above are true or false

## Connection between Math, Logic, Philosophy, and Linguistics
The connection of all four of these fields can be connected through propositions. Logic deals with reasoning and arguments making it the basis of studying mathematical proofs, but logic is not limited to propositions in mathematics. Philosophy of language and linguistics studies what propositions can mean, how they can be interpreted, how truth changes between interpretations, what truth is, and the [[Sense & Reference|Sense]] behind a statement.
